# Paper

A paper ad shows paper people living and doing things in a world made entirely
of paper. The whole environment and every object in it is paper: card, folds,
cut layers, glue seams. It is photographed for real, so it has grain and real
shadows, not a flat digital drawing.

What makes it read as paper:
- Paper texture on everything: grain, fibres, cut or torn edges that show the
  paper's thickness, soft shadows between layers.
- The camera inside the paper world. The words "diorama", "shadow box",
  "tabletop" and "model" show the craft object from outside (a box on a desk),
  so leave them out.
- Say "papercraft", not "paper cut", near faces and labels; "paper cut" blurs
  them. Say "paper stop motion", never "stop motion" alone, which drifts to clay.
- Flat movement: pieces slide and pivot. A slow push through the paper layers is
  the move that works best. Jointed paper limbs get smoothed into rubber, and
  pencil lines that draw themselves fail, so avoid both.

The product is paper too, with its real label.

Examples:
- "Papercraft scene: a paper girl in a folded card raincoat walks down a street
  of layered cardstock houses; cut edges show the card's thickness; paper rain
  hangs on threads; soft light, camera at her eye level inside the paper world."
- "Papercraft scene: a juice bottle built from green card with its real label,
  standing among pleated paper oranges on a paper kitchen counter; shallow depth
  of field."
- Video: "Paper stop-motion cadence: the camera pushes slowly through the
  layers of paper houses towards the bottle."

Any variation the user asks for (origami, flat cutout, collage) is fine; keep it all paper.
